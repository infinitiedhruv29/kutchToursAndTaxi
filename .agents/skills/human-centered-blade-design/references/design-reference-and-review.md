# Design reference and review guide

Use this as a practical companion to `SKILL.md`.

## Reference research starting points

Use these as places to discover patterns, not as automatic templates:

- Awwwards — https://www.awwwards.com/
- Dribbble — https://dribbble.com/
- Behance — https://www.behance.net/
- Pinterest — https://www.pinterest.com/
- Land-book — https://land-book.com/
- Godly — https://godly.website/

Also search for respected live websites in the exact business category. Live websites are particularly useful for navigation, content, trust, mobile usability, and realistic customer journeys; gallery shots are often more art-directed than practical.

## How to evaluate a reference

For each useful reference, note:

1. What is the business and intended audience?
2. What works: typography, hierarchy, layout, imagery, navigation, or interaction?
3. What does not transfer to our project?
4. What can be adapted without copying the brand or page?
5. Does the pattern remain useful on mobile?
6. Does it make the customer task easier?

Do not use a design reference simply because it is trendy or visually impressive.

## Fast visual QA

Review the page at:
- Narrow mobile: approximately 360–390 CSS pixels.
- Larger mobile/tablet: approximately 768 CSS pixels.
- Desktop: approximately 1280–1440 CSS pixels.

Check:
- No horizontal scrolling unless the content pattern intentionally requires it.
- Headings wrap naturally and do not dominate the whole screen.
- Main actions are clear and do not compete with one another.
- Cards, borders, shadows, and corner radii are consistent.
- Images are relevant and cropped intentionally.
- Text has comfortable contrast and line length.
- Forms show labels, validation, and focus states.
- Navigation, buttons, links, and menus work.
- The page still resembles the business it serves, not a generic UI kit.
